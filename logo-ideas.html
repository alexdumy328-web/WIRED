Wired / wordmark study

The name is the logo.

Simple, modern directions built around the word “Wired.” No separate symbol required. Recreate the strongest one as vector text in Inkscape.

01 / Clean accentBest all-rounder
Wired.

One-color wordmark + signal dot

Keep the letters untouched and use the dot as the only brand detail. The dot can become lime, amber, or violet depending on the application.

02 / Split signalMore character
Wired

Break the word at the connection

A small colored point separates “Wire” from “d,” suggesting a connection without adding an icon or making the mark busy.

03 / Technical monoWorkshop feel
Wired_

Use a restrained monospace

The underscore makes it feel like a technical label or diagnostic tool. Best for stickers, invoices, and a more hands-on installation identity.

04 / OutlineNight-sign version
Wired.

Outline the letters, not the idea

A thin outline gives the wordmark a subtle automotive-display feel and works well as a one-color vinyl cut or illuminated sign.

05 / Route WCustom-letter option
ired.

Customize only the first letter

Keep “ired.” as normal text and redraw the W as one continuous route. This is the most ownable option, but it needs careful manual vector work.

06 / InverseDark-mode signature
Wired.

Strong black-and-white fallback

Use this as the master reference: if the logo works in black on white and white on black, it will survive every future color change.

My recommendation: start with concept 01 for the main brand, then explore a custom W from concept 05 only if you want a more distinctive version. In Inkscape, type “Wired” first, convert the text to a path, adjust the letter spacing, and keep the dot as the only accent.
